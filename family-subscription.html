Case study · Ynmo · Web

Family subscription

I designed how families subscribe to a center in Ynmo’s marketplace, then audited my own design with the Six Minds framework, one mind at a time.

Role

Product Designer, owned the flow

Team

Me, 1 PM

Method

Six Minds self-audit

Status

Handed off, release postponed

Context

Parents search for a center, open its profile to see programs and plans, then subscribe. I had no access to parents, so I couldn’t test the flow.

I still wanted a structured check before handoff, not just another look at my own screens.

Six passes, one mind at a time

  • Vision: is the most important thing the most visible?
  • Wayfinding: can parents tell where they are and how to get back?
  • Language: are labels in the parents’ words, and consistent?
  • Memory: do parents have to remember anything between screens?
  • Decision making: is the main action obvious?
  • Emotion: how does it feel when something goes wrong?

Findings

13

issues across 5 minds, all fixed in the design.

Vision 5

  • Center name raised from 18 to 20 for stronger hierarchy
  • Active filters no longer look like the Search button
  • Unselected filter options from green to grey
  • Less contrast on unselected profile tabs
  • Only the old price is crossed out, not its label

Wayfinding 4

  • On-screen back to the listing
  • On-screen back to the center profile
  • A way to go back and edit entered data
  • A line under the selected tab

Language 2

  • An English review inside the Arabic interface
  • One word for subscribe (اشتراك) everywhere

Memory 1

  • Center logo and name shown during registration

Emotion 1

  • A helpful empty state when there are no results

Decision making 0

  • No issues found

Reflection

What surprised me

Basic things were missing from my own design, like an on-screen back button. A structured pass catches what you’ve stopped seeing.

Limits and next step

It shows where a parent might struggle, not where they do. Next: test the top fixes with 5 parents.
